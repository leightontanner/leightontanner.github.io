I am a philosopher of science and technology working in the pragmatist tradition. My research centers on the philosophy and ethics of artificial intelligence, and I also work in the philosophy of language and the foundations of quantum theory. I received my PhD in History and Philosophy of Science from the University of Pittsburgh in May 2026, under the supervision of David Wallace. I am currently a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Department of Philosophy at Texas A&M University.

In my work on AI, I argue that debates about large language models often run together four questions: what capacities a system has, what its outputs mean, what position an output occupies in the practice that receives it, and what status the system itself holds there. Influential arguments that these systems lack communicative intentions, or that their outputs are meaningless, answer the first two, but neither settles the last two, on which reliance and responsibility turn. On my account, an output can be answerable to the world even though the system that produced it cannot answer for it. What makes the output answerable is an arrangement that can test it against evidence and correct it, whether or not anyone asserted it. The system cannot answer for it, because current models are norm-sensitive without being norm-responsible: they lack the persistence, sanctionability, and stake in their own standing that responsibility requires. Answering for their outputs therefore falls to whoever authorizes reliance on them, and I defend an Authorization Principle that says what such authorizers owe: proportionate checks, a responsible owner, and a way of telling checked material from unchecked. I am developing this work into a book, Machine Intelligence and Moral Responsibility: A Pragmatist Account.

Across these projects, I use a method I call irenic pragmatism, a methodologically conservative form of the neo-pragmatism of Rorty, Brandom, and Price. It starts from the practice-level commitments that rival views share and takes on further semantic or metaphysical commitments only when a particular task requires them. I first developed it in the philosophy of science, where I defend a pragmatist interpretation of quantum theory.

I also hold a B.S. in Physics and Philosophy from the University of Minnesota and have programming experience in Python, C++, and MATLAB.
